Overview

How to practice each task

Short daily sessions improve your tone, speed and clarity.

Listen and Repeat

Listen to short sentences and repeat them aloud.
Focus on stress, rhythm and intonation.
Record yourself and compare with the native audio.
Try shadowing exercises for fluency.

Speaking Interview

Answer short, real-life questions on the spot.
Put your ideas in one or two clear sentences.
Use connectors: because, so, for example.
Speak naturally; do not memorize scripts.
Practice with a timer and listen to high-scoring samples.
Remember: consistency matters more than long sessions.

Sentences

From short to long

The sentences grow in length and complexity.
These examples come from official questions.

Sentences 1–3 Short

5–10 words
Simple commands: one action, one object.
“Start by selecting a bicycle from the rack.”
“Insert a coffee filter into the slot.”
“Use the dumbbells for strength-building exercises.”

Sentences 4–5 Medium

10–14 words
Commands with a purpose or detail, often with “in order to” or “before / after”.
“Always wear protective goggles in order to shield your eyes.”
“Perform stretching exercises on the mat to avoid injury.”
“Confirm the total before finishing the transaction.”

Sentences 6–7 Long

15–20+ words
Two clauses, “if” structures, or several instructions joined together.
“The elevators will not operate during an emergency drill, so take the stairs instead.”
“For the best overall performance, you will need to clean your coffee machine at least once each month.”
Why it matters: in sentences 1–3 you can score 5 easily.
Sentences 6–7 are where most students lose points, so practice longer sentences the most.
Scoring

Scoring from 1 to 5

Each score describes how accurately and clearly you repeat the sentences.

ScoreIn shortWhat it sounds like
5Clear, complete, exactAll the words and the meaning, with natural pronunciation and rhythm. Easy for any listener to understand.
4Same meaning, not word for wordYou may forget one or two small words or use a synonym. Small grammar or pronunciation slips do not change the meaning. You may pause or self-correct once.
3Most of it, meaning not fully accurateYou repeat most important words but change or leave out a few that matter. Pronunciation causes some difficulty.
2Many important words missingYou often repeat only part of the sentence or change many key words. It sounds incomplete or has unrelated words.
1Very little, mostly unclearOnly a few words, and most of the meaning is missing. Some attempts are recognizable but hard to understand.
Tip: you can still earn 4 out of 5 with the same meaning, even if you forget a few words or use synonyms.
Changing or skipping key words that change the message lowers your score.
Scoring

What each score looks like

The meaning is what matters most.

Example 1

Prompt: “You can prevent messy spills and splashes by not overfilling the water tank.”
Score 5 The exact sentence.
Score 4 “You can avoid messy spills by not overfilling the water tank.”
Same meaning, slightly shorter.
Score 2–3 “You can… spills… by not filling the water.”
Key words are missing and the meaning is unclear.

Example 2

Prompt: “Complete a safety check and fix any problems before beginning your trip.”
Score 4 “Do a safety check and fix any issues before starting your trip.”
Different words, same meaning.
 
Remember: slightly different words are fine if the meaning stays the same.
If the meaning changes or is incomplete, your score drops.
Strategies

Strategies to improve listening

What to do while you listen.

1 · Listen for chunks

English is spoken in small groups of words.
Obey every road sign / and keep in mind / that a helmet must be worn / at all times.
Remember each chunk, not each word.

2 · Action + object first

Find what to do and what it applies to.
Scan (action) the barcode (object) on every item on the belt (detail).

3 · Function words

Small words like is, to, the, by, for are easy to miss.
They matter for grammar. Dropping them changes the meaning or sounds incomplete.

4 · Rhythm and stress

Speakers stress the important words.
SCAN the BARcode for each ITEM on the BELT.
Remembering the rhythm helps you recall the sentence.

5 · Visualize

Imagine yourself doing the action.
Wipe down equipment after use: picture yourself cleaning a gym machine.
The mental image helps your memory.

6 · Expect longer endings

Sentences 6 and 7 have two parts.
Listen for the connecting word (and, so, if, before).
Remember each part separately.
Strategies

Strategies for clear repetition

What to do while you speak.

1 · Begin right away

Start confidently after the tone.
Do not wait too long or fill the time with “uh” or “um”.

2 · Keep the same meaning

Forget a word? Use another word that means the same.
resolve any issues → fix any problems

3 · Complete sentences

Do not stop halfway.
If you forget something in the middle, finish the sentence naturally and include the ending.

4 · Clear endings

Say every syllable, especially the endings -s, -ed, -ing, -tion.
Exercises has four syllables. Required has three.

5 · Same rhythm and pauses

Use the same stress pattern as the speaker.
If the speaker pauses between two clauses, pause there too.

6 · Long sentences

If you lose a word in the middle, keep going.
The beginning (the action) and the end (the purpose or result) carry the most meaning.

4Check the status lights to confirm the router is working.Medium
5Sign in to the system using your computer’s keyboard.Medium
