Scoring rubrics (1 to 5) — teacher only

How to score. Type a score in the box next to each answer during the lesson. Write feedback (one thing done well, one thing to improve) in the Notes box. At the end of the lesson click Save Progress: you get a PDF report and a small progress file. Send both to the student. The student loads progress files on the Progress page to see all lessons together. Nothing is stored on your computer.
Listen and Repeat (each sentence)
ScoreWhat you hear
5Every word, natural pronunciation and rhythm. Easy to understand for any listener.
4Same meaning as the original, even if not word for word. One or two small words missed or replaced by a synonym. Small grammar or pronunciation slips do not change the meaning. At most one pause or self-correction.
3Most of the sentence, but the meaning is not completely accurate: a few important words changed or left out. Pronunciation causes some difficulty, but the general idea is clear.
2Many important words missing or changed. Only part of the sentence, or unrelated words.
1Very little of the sentence, or mostly unclear. Most of the meaning is missing.

Example: prompt “Complete a safety check and fix any problems before beginning your trip.” Answer “Do a safety check and fix any issues before starting your trip.” = 4 (same meaning).

Take an Interview (each answer)
ScoreWhat you hear
5Fully developed and very clear. A strong main idea with logical reasons and specific examples. Accurate, effective grammar and vocabulary. Natural pronunciation, rhythm and intonation.
4Answers the question directly and is well organized. A reason and a simple example. Mostly accurate and clear language. Easy to follow, natural pace.
3Generally understandable and somewhat organized. A clear main idea, but development is limited or uneven. Some errors distract. May be too short or not support the opinion fully.
2Some connection to the question but important parts missing. Ideas not well developed or not connected. Frequent language problems make it hard to follow.
1Mostly unclear or not connected to the question. Very few ideas; very short or hard to understand.

A simple, complete, organized answer can still earn a high score.

Writing: Academic Discussion response
ScoreWhat you read
5Clear position, logical reasons with specific examples, responds to a classmate’s idea, connectors used well, accurate sentences.
4Clear position with a reason and an example. Mostly accurate. Small errors do not change the meaning.
3Understandable and somewhat organized. Limited development. Errors sometimes distract.
2Some connection to the task but incomplete ideas, little organization, frequent errors.
1Mostly unclear or off topic. Very few ideas.

These descriptions are for classroom feedback. They are not official ETS score bands.

Lesson 1 — Speaking Part 1: Listen and Repeat

Round 1 — sentences 1–3
  1. Press the green button / to begin.
  2. Wipe down the bench / after each use.
  3. Scan the barcode / on every item.
Round 2 — sentences 4–5
  1. Choose your classes carefully / in order to finish your schedule / before Friday.
  2. Wipe the lab bench / with a damp cloth / before you leave.
Round 3 — sentences 6–7
  1. The fire alarm will sound twice, / so walk to the designated area / and wait for instructions.
  2. If the screen freezes / while you are registering for classes, / log out / and try again / after a few minutes.

Slashes show the chunks. Read each sentence once at natural speed. The student repeats. Then the student clicks Show and compares. You type the score (1 to 5) in the box next to each sentence.

  1. after a few minutes
  2. try again after a few minutes
  3. log out and try again after a few minutes
  4. If the screen freezes while you are registering for classes, log out and try again after a few minutes.
Slide: Chunk by chunk (say each chunk; the student repeats)
  1. The tour guide
  2. will give you a headset
  3. before the tour starts.
  4. The tour guide will give you a headset before the tour starts.
Slide: Memory ladder (read each sentence once; the student repeats; you score)
  1. Plug in the router.
  2. Plug in the router and wait for the light.

Round 4 — practice set (BestMyTest)

The student opens the first practice set link and repeats every sentence. You listen and type a score (1 to 5) for each of the 7 sentences in the boxes on the slide. The second practice set is the homework.

Answer keys

Quick review (Lesson 0): 7 sentences, 8–12 seconds · device/app, facility, workplace, campus · short (5–10 words) to long (15–20+) · scored 1 to 5, same meaning can be a 4 · sentences 6–7 · start right away, finish the sentence, word endings, rhythm

What score would you give? Example 5 · 1 = 4 (same meaning) · 2 = 2 (many words missing) · 3 = 3 (head changes the meaning) · 4 = 4 (one pause, same meaning)

Chunks: Press the green button / to begin. · Wipe the lab bench / with a damp cloth / before you leave. · The fire alarm will sound twice, / so walk to the designated area / and wait for instructions. · If the screen freezes while you are registering for classes, / log out and try again / after a few minutes.

Action + object: Scan, barcode · Insert, coffee filter

Which word fits? Example goggles · 1 cable · 2 filter · 3 barcode · 4 alarm

What changed? 1 Wrong ending · 2 Missing word · 3 Changed word · 4 Word order

Homework 3 (picture words): router (example) · cable, headphones, treadmill, dumbbell, goggles, microscope

Homework 4 (chunks in order): Fill the water tank before you turn on the machine. · Always keep your receipt in case you need to return an item. · After you finish the experiment, clean the equipment and wash your hands.

Homework 5 (action and object): filter, slot · goggles, shield · area, alarm · bench, cloth

Listen for while the student repeats
  • Missing or changed small words (the, a, to)
  • Word endings: -s, -ed
  • Stress on key words; steady rhythm
  • Silence in the middle of the sentence

Lesson 2 — From Repeating to Answering

Slide: Warm-up (read each sentence once; the student repeats)
  1. I usually study at the library because it is quiet.
  2. I want to join the debate club, so I will email the president.
  3. For example, last week I finished my report in the study room.
Slide: Try it (read aloud)
  1. Where do you usually study, and why?
  2. What do you usually do on weekends?

Connectors to find: because · so · For example

Homework 2: so, because, for example, but, because, so

Lesson 3 — Speaking Part 2: Take an Interview

Slides: Interview practice 1 and 2 (read each question aloud)
  1. How do you usually prepare for a big exam?
  2. Tell me about a teacher who helped you.
  3. Do you think students should work part-time while they study? Why?
  4. What is one skill you want to improve this year, and how will you practice it?

Listen for: a direct answer first, a reason, an example, a wrap-up, connectors, steady pace. Record every answer.

Answer keys

Question types: 1 Personal experience · 2 Opinion · 3 Habit or routine · 4 Future plan · 5 Personal experience · 6 Opinion

Answer structure: Answer → Reason → Example → Wrap up (the four sentences in that order).

Weak or strong: Answer B · It has a reason, an example and a wrap-up · A reason and an example

Connector jobs: The main reason is that = Reason · For instance = Example · However = Contrast · In short = Wrap up · Another reason is that = Reason · On the other hand = Contrast · To sum up = Wrap up · For example = Example

Keep talking: Well, let me think about that · What I mean is… · Another reason is that… · In short, …

Homework 2 (gap-fill): reason, example, instance, however, short, Another

Lesson 4 — Writing

Build a Sentence (key)

Practice 1

  1. The professor asked us to submit the report by Monday.
  2. Could you tell me where the registration office is?
  3. I have been studying English since I was twelve.

Practice 2

  1. The results of the experiment were published last year.
  2. She wanted to know if the library was open on weekends.
  3. If I had more time, I would join the debate club.

Homework 3 (typed sentences)

  1. Students must register before the deadline.
  2. I would like to know when the library opens.
  3. The article that we read last week was very useful.
Model answer parts: Position · Reason · Example · Reply to a classmate · Wrap up
Connectors: agree, however, instance, addition, result, conclusion
Make it better: 1 Many people like technology because it is useful in daily life. · 2 She has studied English for three years. · 3 For example, last month I finished a group project with students from four countries.
Feedback criteria (match the tracker)

Sentence structure · Organization · Relevance and development · Cohesion. Score each from 1 to 6 in the progress report.
